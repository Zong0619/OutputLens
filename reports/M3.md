## Architecture Impact

### Validated

- **Independent axes (Principle 13)**: A4 and A5 run in parallel with no mutual
  dependencies, validating axis independence.
- **Classification layer**: Four analyzers (A4, A5, A9, A10) execute correctly
  in their specified layers (classification, synthesis).
- **Pipeline depth**: Full 7-analyzer pipeline operational: A1→A2→A3→{A4,A5}→{A9,A10}
- **Signal-based reasoning**: Both A4 and A5 produce reasoning explaining
  detected signals rather than restating classification labels.

### No Impact

- A6 (Novelty), A7-A8 (Structure), A11-A16 (remaining synthesis/terminal) not yet tested

## Specification Validation

### Requirements Validated

- Classification output formats: EstablishednessAnnotation, EvidenceAnnotation,
  TrustProfile, EvidenceGapReport all produced correctly
- Reasoning requirement (min 20 chars): Enforced at the type level
- Classification scales: E1-E5, R1-R4 levels correctly assigned

### Ambiguities Discovered

- **E1/E2 boundary**: Depends on A3 concept domain associations. Without
  domain-aware concepts, common knowledge defaults to E3.
- **R4/R3 boundary**: Evidence gesture + specific statistic → R4 escalation.
  This heuristic is explicitly documented.

## Lessons Learned

1. **A5 is more tractable than A4**: Evidence presence/absence is detectable
   from text patterns. Establishedness requires knowledge about what is
   "common" vs. "domain" knowledge. This validates the specification's
   independent-axes design.
2. **Reasoning quality matters more than classification accuracy**: The
   evaluation examples show that signal-based reasoning ("detected:
   specific_statistic, no citation present") is more useful to readers than a
   naked classification label. This confirms the specification's reasoning
   requirement (Principle 12).
